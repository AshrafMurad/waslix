# Design

<!-- impeccable:design-schema 1 -->

## Visual World

Waslix extends its product workspace into an evidence-to-action operations rail for marketing surfaces. Customer facts visibly move from signal to health, owner, and next action inside the same compact, accountable visual language as the dashboard rather than a separate promotional theme.

## Palette

Marketing surfaces use the application tokens directly: neutral background, white surface, raised neutral, teal identity and interaction, and green, amber, and red only for semantic state. Color indicates state or command emphasis; health, attention, and risk remain distinct from the Waslix brand teal in light and dark themes.

## Components

Primary marketing controls inherit dashboard geometry, borders, density, and state behavior. Product demonstrations show source evidence, score impact, owner, due window, and next action together. On narrow screens these facts stack rather than crop or disappear. Avoid floating KPI cards that do not preserve evidence or ownership context.

## Typography

Use the existing application font stack with tabular numerals for operational data. Marketing display type is large, direct, and tightly composed without gradient text. Labels use uppercase tracking only for short operational tags.

## Motion

Motion expresses work moving through a system: a bounded Three.js signal field carries evidence behind the opening workspace, continuity rails connect sections, and selected account states settle with Motion. Keep content visible by default and disable nonessential motion under reduced-motion preferences.

## Responsive And RTL

English and Arabic are equal surfaces. Layout uses logical properties and preserves semantic order in RTL. The dispatch board collapses from rail columns to stacked strips on narrow screens without hiding the evidence, owner, or next action.

## Prohibitions

Do not use generic neon gradients, glass panels as decoration, fabricated AI claims, invented customer proof, or oversized metric-card hero templates. Do not present planned integrations as live capabilities.
